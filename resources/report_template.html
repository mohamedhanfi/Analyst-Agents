Executive Summary

Executive readout
Revenue grew 27.4% — sustainably — as more customers (▲12%) bought bigger baskets (▲10%). Growth is concentrated and exposure asymmetric: one category now drives nearly 40% of sales.
  1. 1.01 Growth is real and durable. Revenue rose from $1.92M to $2.45M, driven simultaneously by order volume and average order value — a structurally healthier pattern than single-lever growth.
  2. 1.02 Electronics is the engine. +34% brings Electronics to 38% of revenue, ahead of Home & Garden (26%) and Apparel (21%).
  3. 1.03 Risk is concentrated. Two categories and one channel each represent 64% of revenue — efficient today, fragile under shock.
  4. 1.04 Basket size is the main lever. The strongest signal in the data links items-per-order to revenue (r = 0.85).
  5. 1.05 Attribution is pending. Promotion and seasonality effects cannot be isolated without campaign data — see Section 6.

Findings

Interpretations grounded in the figures above.

Electronics moves the P&L
High confidence

Electronics grew 34% and now constitutes 38% of total revenue — more than Home & Garden (26%) and Apparel (21%) combined. Pricing, stock, or supply decisions at this level move the headline figure directly.

Q4 is real — volume and value grew together

Revenue rose 27.4% through simultaneous growth in orders (▲12%) and average order value (▲10%). Dual-lever growth is more robust than growth from a single lever.

Honest limit: we can see the jump; we cannot yet prove its cause.

Basket size, not price, drives growth
Medium confidence

The strongest correlation pair is items-per-order → revenue (r = 0.85), pointing to bundling, free-shipping thresholds, and cross-sell before any price change.

Concentration is a structural risk
High confidence

Two categories and one channel (online) each represent 64% of revenue. Not adverse today — but the single-category buffer is thin, and a shock would hit a large share at once.

Issues & Recommended Actions

Why each issue matters, plus the first concrete step.

5.1 · Over-reliance on Electronics + Home (64% of revenue)

Why it matters: a disruption in the core category reduces 38% of revenue at once.

Action — trial an assortment refresh in Apparel for 90 days; compare category share before and after.

5.2 · The cause of the Q4 acceleration is not yet attributable

Why it matters: without attribution the Q4 result cannot be reproduced deliberately.

Action — record promotion/campaign flags so future reports compare "with-promo vs without-promo".

5.3 · No customer-level visibility (untapped upside)

Why it matters: repeat-purchase, at-risk and recommendation models require per-customer data, which is not collected today.

Action — include customer IDs and demographic fields in the next export.

5.4 · Address and size fields have gaps

Why it matters: 45 orders lack a city, so geography-level claims are unreliable.

Action — make city mandatory at checkout; treat regional claims as approximate until resolved.
